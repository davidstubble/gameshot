# GameShot LABS Pricing Research — 2026-10-15

## Verified public comparisons

- **Adobe Express:** official pricing page shows Free at US$0 and Premium at US$9.99/month. Premium includes more templates/assets, additional editing tools, more storage, scheduling, version history, one-click resize, and brand management. Source: https://www.adobe.com/express/pricing
- **Canva:** official pricing page shows Pro at US$144/year for one person and Business at US$250/year per person. Pro includes premium content, AI tools, social scheduling, brand kits, storage, and other design tools. Source: https://www.canva.com/pricing/
- **TinyWow:** official site positions more than 200 tools across PDF, image, video, file, and AI categories. It presents free tools and a premium offer with no ads, unlimited usage, and faster processing; the page shows $9/month in one section and a checkout panel showing $20 monthly or $15/month on yearly billing, so the site has inconsistent visible pricing and should not be used as a precise benchmark without checkout verification. Source: https://tinywow.com/
- **Batch Image Crop:** official site is free, requires no signup, processes locally in the browser, and offers batch crop modes, social presets, ZIP export, shape crop, and no-upload privacy claims. It also shows an optional Buy Me a Coffee link rather than a required paid plan. Source: https://batchimagecrop.com/

## Market signal

The comparable market spans free local utilities, broad creative subscriptions around $9.99/month, and larger annual suites. GameShot LABS should not try to win by matching Canva or Adobe feature-for-feature. Its differentiators are ordered batch handoff, local-first processing, exact pixel controls, and practical exports.

## Pricing implications

1. Keep a meaningful free tier because free local competitors exist.
2. Charge for workflow scale, saved local history, reusable presets, creator delivery packs, and privacy/layout batches rather than for basic crop safety.
3. Use a low-friction one-time purchase for occasional users and a separate recurring plan only when ongoing value exists.
4. Use fixed product/price IDs, not arbitrary client-controlled dynamic prices.
5. Test web pricing by cohort only after entitlements, refunds, and analytics are reliable; configure Apple price tiers separately.
6. Do not change current live prices until the owner approves an exact offer and the payment products are configured in the correct environment.
